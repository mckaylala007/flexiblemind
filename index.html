    Heavy weather

    Some things aren't nutrients or toxins. Shame, grief, fear, loneliness — these are weather every human passes through. They aren't your fault, and they aren't a habit to break. Weather asks for care, not correction.

    Shame

    The feeling that something is wrong with you — not what you did, but what you are. Everyone carries it sometimes; it just convinces each of us we're the only one.
    Care: shame shrinks in the light. Tell one safe person the thing. Stand on God's grace and self-forgiveness — shame can't survive being truly known and still loved.
    When it lingers, it often fuels perfectionism and isolation — worth untangling with support.

    Grief

    Waves, not stages. Missing what's been lost — a person, a season, a version of your life you thought you'd have.
    Care: let it move through you; grieving is how acceptance happens. No timeline, no guilt for the good days or the bad ones.
    Grief has no deadline — but you don't have to carry it alone.

    Fear

    The body bracing. Wanting to shrink, stall, or run — even when nothing's chasing you today.
    Care: fear is a signal, not a command. Courage isn't fear's absence — it's action with fear still in the passenger seat. Move in inches.
    When fear starts running your daily decisions, support can loosen its grip.

    Not enough

    Measuring yourself and coming up short — no matter what the evidence says.
    Care: check whose scale you're standing on. God's view of you over the world's. This feeling lies with total confidence — it's weather, not a verdict.
    If the scale never lets you off, that's worth talking through with someone.

    Loneliness

    Alone even in full rooms. Unseen. The ache of connection missing, whatever the company.
    Care: loneliness is a signal for connection, not proof you're unlovable. One text. Small doses of support, repleted slowly.
    Chip away at it before isolation cements.

    Anxiety

    The body on alert with nowhere to aim it. Worry with a motor — chest tight, mind sprinting.
    Care: ground the body first — breath, a walk, sunlight, forward motion. Then challenge the thought: is this 100% true?
    Persistent anxiety deserves real support. A therapist is a nutrient, not a last resort.

    Depression

    Stagnancy. Colors muted, everything heavy and far away. Even the things you love arriving flat.
    Care: shrink the day. One nutrient at a time — sunlight, forward motion, one text sent. This is weather, not weakness, and gentleness counts double here.
    If the weather won't lift, bring in support — reaching for help is strength, not defeat.
    All weather passes differently. If a season won't lift, support isn't a last resort — it's a nutrient.
